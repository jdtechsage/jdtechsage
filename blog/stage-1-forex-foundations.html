Chapter 1
What is the Forex Market?
The world's largest financial market — and why retail traders are the smallest players

Forex (foreign exchange) is the global market where currencies are bought and sold. Unlike stocks, there is no central exchange — it runs 24 hours a day, 5 days a week, across four major financial centers: Sydney, Tokyo, London, and New York.

When you travel abroad and exchange dollars for euros, you're participating in forex. Traders do the same thing, but speculate on whether one currency will rise or fall in value relative to another.

🌍
$7.5 Trillion Daily
More money trades hands in forex every single day than the entire US stock market does in a month. It's the most liquid market on Earth — you can enter or exit almost any position instantly.
🕐
24 Hours, 5 Days
Forex never sleeps on weekdays. When New York closes, Sydney opens. This means you can trade early morning, lunch, or late at night — but not all hours are equal.
🏦
Decentralized Market
No single exchange controls forex. Banks, institutions, and brokers trade directly with each other (OTC — Over the Counter). Your broker gives you access to this network.
📱
Retail Access
Before the internet, only banks could trade forex. Now, with $100 and a phone, anyone can access the same markets. This is both an opportunity and a warning.
⚡ Common Mistake — Thinking it's like stocks Stocks = you own part of a company. Forex = you're betting on one currency's value vs another. There are no dividends, no earnings reports, no "company fundamentals" to study. The drivers are different: interest rates, inflation, geopolitics, and economic data.

Why Do Exchange Rates Move?

Every country's currency has a relative value to others. This value changes constantly based on:

  • Interest rates: Higher rates attract foreign investment → stronger currency. When the US Fed raises rates, USD often strengthens.
  • Economic data: Strong GDP, low unemployment, high exports → currency tends to rise.
  • Inflation: High inflation erodes purchasing power → currency weakens over time.
  • Geopolitical events: War, elections, trade disputes → sudden sharp moves in related currencies.
  • Market sentiment: Risk-on = traders buy riskier currencies (AUD, NZD). Risk-off = flee to safe havens (JPY, CHF, USD).
ℹ️ Real Example In March 2020 (COVID crash), the Japanese Yen (JPY) and US Dollar (USD) both surged while the Australian Dollar (AUD) fell sharply. Why? Investors fled to "safe haven" currencies. This risk-on/risk-off behavior is one of the most consistent patterns in forex.
Chapter 2
Currency Pairs Explained
Majors, minors, exotics — and how to read a price quote

Forex always trades in pairs. You're always buying one currency and selling another at the same time. A pair like EUR/USD means: how many US Dollars does one Euro cost?

📖 Reading a Currency Pair EUR/USD = 1.0842 means: 1 Euro costs $1.0842 US Dollars.
The LEFT currency (EUR) is the base. The RIGHT (USD) is the quote.
If EUR/USD goes UP → Euro is getting stronger vs Dollar.
If EUR/USD goes DOWN → Euro is getting weaker vs Dollar (or Dollar is strengthening).

Three Categories

⭐
Major Pairs
Always include USD. EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, USD/CHF, AUD/USD, USD/CAD, NZD/USD. Tightest spreads, most liquidity, most predictable. Start here as a beginner.
🔵
Minor / Cross Pairs
Major currencies but without USD. EUR/GBP, EUR/JPY, GBP/JPY. Slightly wider spreads. GBP/JPY ("The Dragon") is known for extreme volatility — not for beginners.
⚠️
Exotic Pairs
USD paired with emerging market currencies: USD/TRY, USD/ZAR, USD/MXN. Very wide spreads, high volatility, low liquidity. Avoid until experienced.
🥇
Recommended Starter Pairs
EUR/USD — most traded, tightest spreads, tons of analysis available.
GBP/USD — volatile but readable.
Stick to 1–2 pairs when learning.
✓ Rule As a beginner, trade EUR/USD only. You don't need to trade 20 pairs. Mastering one pair means you understand its behavior, its key levels, which sessions it moves most, and which news events affect it. Depth over breadth.
Chapter 3
Pips, Lots & Position Sizing
The math that determines every dollar you make or lose

A pip (Percentage In Point) is the smallest standard price movement in forex. For most pairs like EUR/USD, 1 pip = 0.0001 (the 4th decimal place). For USD/JPY, 1 pip = 0.01 (the 2nd decimal place, since JPY has fewer decimals).

📐
What is a Pip?
EUR/USD moves from 1.0842 → 1.0843 = 1 pip.
EUR/USD moves from 1.0800 → 1.0900 = 100 pips.
Many brokers show a 5th decimal — that's a pipette (0.1 pip).
📦
Lot Sizes
Standard lot: 100,000 units — 1 pip ≈ $10
Mini lot: 10,000 units — 1 pip ≈ $1
Micro lot: 1,000 units — 1 pip ≈ $0.10
Nano lot: 100 units — 1 pip ≈ $0.01

⚡ Common Mistake — Wrong lot size A beginner opens a $500 account and trades 1 standard lot. That's 100,000 units. A 20-pip move against them = $200 loss — wiping 40% of their account. Always calculate position size BEFORE entering any trade.
💡 Beginner Rule Start with micro lots (0.01). A $200 loss on a bad trade when you're micro trading = just $2. You learn the same lesson without the financial damage. Scale up only after you're consistently profitable on demo.

Market Sessions & Trading Hours
Not all hours are equal — when to trade and when to stay out

Forex runs 24/5, but activity and volatility vary dramatically by hour. Trading at 3 AM when only one market is open is like fishing in a near-empty pond. The real action happens when sessions overlap.

JPY pairs (USD/JPY, EUR/JPY) see their highest activity here. Market tends to set ranges that London later breaks. Bank of Japan influence is strongest.
🏦 London Session
07:00–16:00 GMT
Highest volume session. European banks open, GBP and EUR pairs become extremely active. Most significant price moves of the day begin here. Beginners should focus on this session.
🗽 New York Session
12:00–21:00 GMT
Second largest. USD pairs dominate. The London–NY overlap (12:00–17:00) is the single most liquid period in forex — tightest spreads, biggest moves. Major US news releases hit here (NFP, CPI).
✓ When to Trade as a Beginner London session open (07:00–10:00 GMT) and London–NY overlap (13:00–17:00 GMT) are the two best windows. This is when institutional money is active, liquidity is highest, setups are cleanest, and spreads are smallest.
Chapter 5
Who Moves the Market?
Understanding the hierarchy — and where retail traders sit in it

The forex market is not fair. Different participants have vastly different access, information, and power. Understanding this hierarchy is crucial — retail traders who don't understand it trade against participants who have much more information and capital.

🏛️
Central Banks
Most Powerful
Federal Reserve (USD), ECB (EUR), Bank of Japan (JPY). They SET interest rates and print money. A single statement from the Fed Chair can move EUR/USD 200 pips in minutes. They are the ultimate market movers.
🏦
Commercial Banks
~40% of Volume
JP Morgan, Deutsche Bank, Citi, Goldman Sachs. They trade for clients and themselves. Their order flow creates the institutional footprints (order blocks, fair value gaps) that advanced traders study.
🏢
Hedge Funds
~15% of Volume
Speculative funds trading billions. George Soros famously "broke the Bank of England" in 1992, making $1 billion by shorting the British Pound. These are the smart money participants retail traders try to follow.
🏭
Corporations
Hedging Activity
Apple selling iPhones in Europe receives Euros. They exchange back to USD. These large corporate conversions move currency prices. Not speculative — they just need to exchange currencies for business.
📱
Retail Traders (You)
~5–6% of Volume
Individual traders using apps and platforms. The smallest participants. We cannot move the market — we can only read it and react. The goal is to understand what larger players are doing and trade WITH them, not against them. Retail traders who think they're fighting the market are always on the losing side.
⚡ Common Mistake — Thinking retail opinion moves markets "Everyone on Twitter says EUR/USD is going up" — this means nothing. 10,000 retail traders buying EUR/USD with micro lots have less impact than one medium-sized hedge fund. Don't trade social media sentiment. Trade price and institutional behavior.
Chapter 6
Leverage, Spread & Margin
The double-edged tools every forex trader must understand

Leverage lets you control more money than you have. With 1:100 leverage, $100 controls $10,000. This amplifies both profits AND losses equally. It's the single most dangerous aspect of forex for beginners.

How Leverage Works

Leverage Your Capital Position Size 1% Move (Win) 1% Move (Loss)
1:1 $1,000 $1,000 +$10 -$10 (1%)
1:10 $1,000 $10,000 +$100 -$100 (10%)
1:50 $1,000 $50,000 +$500 -$500 (50%)
1:100 $1,000 $100,000 +$1,000 -$1,000 (WIPED)
⚡ Most Dangerous Mistake in Forex Using maximum leverage as a beginner. A broker offering 1:500 leverage is not doing you a favor — it's giving you a loaded weapon. One 0.2% move against you at 1:500 wipes your account. Experienced prop traders rarely use more than 1:10 to 1:20 effective leverage.

What is Spread?

The spread is the difference between the buy price (ask) and the sell price (bid). This is how brokers make money — it's their fee per trade. Tighter spread = cheaper trading.

✅
Tight Spread (Major Pairs)
EUR/USD: typically 0.1–1 pip spread during London/NY sessions. Very cheap to trade. Scalpers target tight-spread pairs.
⚠️
Wide Spread (Exotic Pairs)
USD/ZAR or USD/TRY: spreads can be 20–80 pips. That means the price needs to move 80 pips in your direction just to break even. Hidden cost of exotics.

What is a Margin Call?

Margin is the deposit required to open a leveraged position. A margin call happens when your losses eat into your required margin, and the broker automatically closes your trades to prevent further loss. It's effectively getting "stopped out" by the broker — usually at the worst possible moment.

✓ Practical Rule on Leverage Use no more than 1:10 effective leverage (better: 1:3 to 1:5). Even if your broker offers 1:500, don't use it. Prop firms typically operate with lower effective leverage built into their risk rules. Your position sizing controls effective leverage — this is where risk management begins.
Chapter 7
Economic News & Forex
The events that move currencies — and when to stay out

Economic data releases are the most powerful short-term movers in forex. When the US jobs report (NFP) is released, EUR/USD can move 100–200 pips in seconds. Understanding what each event means is non-negotiable.

High-Impact News Events

Event Currency Impact What it Measures Direction Rule
NFP (Non-Farm Payrolls) USD HIGH US jobs added each month. Released first Friday of every month. Better than expected → USD rises
CPI (Inflation) USD, EUR, GBP HIGH Consumer Price Index — measures inflation rate High inflation → central bank may raise rates → currency rises
Interest Rate Decision All majors HIGH Central bank sets base interest rate Rate hike → currency strengthens. Rate cut → weakens
GDP Report USD, EUR, GBP HIGH Gross Domestic Product — overall economic growth Strong GDP → currency tends to rise
Retail Sales USD, GBP MED Consumer spending — signals economic health Strong sales → potential rate hike signal → currency up
PMI (Manufacturing) USD, EUR, GBP MED Purchasing Manager Index — factory activity Above 50 = expansion (positive). Below 50 = contraction
FOMC Minutes USD HIGH Federal Reserve meeting notes — hints at future policy Hawkish tone → USD rises. Dovish tone → USD falls
📅 Forex Factory forexfactory.com is the most widely used economic calendar. Check it every morning before trading. Red-folder events = don't trade unless you know exactly what you're doing. Your prop firm may have specific rules about trading during high-impact news.
⚡ Common Mistake — Trading directly INTO news releases NFP is about to release in 5 minutes. You enter a trade because "you think it'll be good." The number comes out better than expected — and EUR/USD drops 80 pips anyway because the market had already priced in a good result. News trading is extremely unpredictable. As a beginner: close positions 30 minutes before major news, wait for the dust to settle, then look for setups.
✓ Beginner News Strategy Don't trade news — trade the aftermath. After NFP, wait 15–30 minutes. Let the spike happen, let the reversal happen, let a new price direction establish. Then trade the continuation with your normal strategy. You avoid the whipsaw and trade a cleaner move.
