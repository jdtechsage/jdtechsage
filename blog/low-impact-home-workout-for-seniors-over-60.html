Low-Impact Home Workout for Seniors Over 60 — No Equipment, 20 Minutes

Gentle, chair-supported exercises to build strength, balance and confidence at home. Always check with your doctor first.

✍️ Deep Darji 📅 Published Oct 24, 2026 🔄 Updated Oct 24, 2026 ⏱ 4 min read 🏷 Seniors • Fitness • Home Workout

A low-impact home workout for seniors over 60 can improve strength, balance and everyday independence without gym equipment or joint-jarring moves. This 20-minute routine uses a sturdy chair, a wall and a counter, and it can be adjusted to your ability.

Regular movement helps protect against muscle loss with age (sarcopenia) and supports mobility. Public health guidance from the World Health Organization recommends that older adults do regular aerobic activity, muscle-strengthening exercise on at least two days a week, and balance training.

Quick answer: Do 6 easy exercises (sit-to-stand, wall push-ups, seated marching, heel raises, leg extensions, balance stance) for 8 to 12 repetitions, 1 to 2 rounds, on 2 to 3 non-consecutive days a week, and walk a little on other days.

Before You Start: Safety First

Medical note: This article is general information, not medical advice. Talk to your doctor before starting a new routine, especially if you have heart conditions, osteoporosis, joint replacements, dizziness or recent surgery. Stop immediately if you feel chest pain, severe breathlessness, dizziness or sharp pain.

  • Wear supportive, non-slip shoes.
  • Use a sturdy chair without wheels, placed against a wall.
  • Keep water nearby and exercise in a well-lit, clutter-free space.
  • Move slowly and breathe steadily. Never hold your breath.

Warm-Up (3 Minutes)

Do shoulder rolls, gentle neck turns, seated ankle circles and slow seated marching for about 30 seconds each.

The 6 Core Exercises

1. Sit-to-Stand (Legs and Hips)

Sit near the front of the chair, feet hip-width apart. Lean slightly forward, stand up using your legs, then lower slowly. Use your hands on the armrests if needed. 8 to 12 reps.

2. Wall Push-Ups (Chest and Arms)

Stand an arm's length from a wall, hands at shoulder height. Bend your elbows to bring your chest toward the wall, then push back. 8 to 10 reps.

3. Seated Marching (Heart and Hip Flexors)

Sit tall and lift one knee at a time as if marching. 30 to 60 seconds.

4. Counter Heel Raises (Calves and Balance)

Hold the kitchen counter, rise onto your toes, pause, and lower slowly. 8 to 12 reps.

5. Seated Leg Extensions (Front of Thigh)

Straighten one leg until it is level with the hip, hold for two seconds, then lower. 8 to 10 reps per leg.

6. Supported Balance Stand (Fall Prevention)

Stand behind the chair holding the back. Place one foot slightly in front of the other (as if on a line) and hold for 10 to 30 seconds. Switch sides. Progress by holding with fewer fingers, only when it feels safe.

Cool-Down (2 Minutes)

Take slow deep breaths and do gentle seated stretches for hamstrings, shoulders and calves. Hold each for 15 to 20 seconds without bouncing.

Tips for Staying Consistent

  • Exercise at the same time each day, such as after morning tea.
  • Track your sessions on a paper calendar.
  • Exercise with a spouse, friend or grandchild for motivation.
  • Eat enough protein and drink water, and ask your doctor about nutrition.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should seniors over 60 exercise?

Public health guidance suggests regular aerobic activity most days, muscle strengthening on at least two days a week, and balance exercises. Build up gradually and follow your doctor's advice.

What is the best exercise for seniors without equipment?

Sit-to-stand, wall push-ups, walking and supported balance exercises are simple, effective options.

Are chair exercises effective?

Yes. Chair-supported exercises can improve strength and balance safely, especially for beginners or people with limited mobility.

Can seniors lift weights at home?

Light resistance such as water bottles or bands can help once basic movements feel easy. Ask a doctor or physiotherapist first.
